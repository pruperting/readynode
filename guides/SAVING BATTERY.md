# Saving your phone's battery

When the power is off and the phone masts are down, your phone's battery is what keeps you in touch: maps, ReadyNet,
the torch, the Meshtastic radio. These steps make one charge last much longer. Do the first four straight away.

On Android, the **ReadyNode** app has a **Save battery** screen (menu → Save battery) that shows how each of these
stands on your phone, with a button to the right setting.

## 1. Aeroplane mode, then Wi-Fi back on

With no phone signal, your phone keeps searching for a mast, turning its radio up to full power to try harder. That
drains the battery fast, for nothing.

1. Turn on **aeroplane mode** (also called flight mode): swipe down from the top of the screen and tap the plane.
2. Turn **Wi-Fi** back on, so you can still use ReadyNet.
3. If you use a Meshtastic radio, turn **Bluetooth** back on too.

Sharing ReadyNet from your phone (its own hotspot) still works in aeroplane mode on the phones we've tried.

Now and then, turn aeroplane mode off for a minute to see if the signal is back. **To call 999**, turn aeroplane mode
off first if your phone doesn't do it for you.

## 2. Battery saver

- **Android:** Settings → Battery → **Battery saver**. Many phones also have a stronger mode that leaves only calls,
  messages and a few apps you choose:
  - Pixel: **Extreme Battery Saver**.
  - Samsung: a stronger power saving mode in Settings → Battery ("Limit apps and Home screen" or "Maximum power saving").
  - Xiaomi, Redmi and Poco: **Ultra battery saver**.
  - OnePlus, Oppo and Realme: **Super power saving mode**.

  If you use the strongest mode, add ReadyNode, Maps and Meshtastic to the apps it allows.
- **iPhone:** Settings → Battery → **Low Power Mode**.

## 3. The screen

The screen uses more power than anything else.

- Turn the **brightness down** as far as you comfortably can.
- Set the screen to switch off after **30 seconds** (Android: Settings → Display → Screen timeout; iPhone: Settings →
  Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock).
- Don't leave a map or video open on the screen when you're not looking at it.

## 4. Location (GPS)

Turn **location off** and only turn it on when you need it: to see where you are, or to follow a map. GPS works with no
signal, but uses power whenever an app asks for it.

## 5. Apps in the background

- For apps you won't need (social media, shopping, games), set their battery use to **Restricted**: Android, Settings →
  Apps → the app → Battery. On iPhone, turn off **Background App Refresh** (Settings → General).
- **Don't use "task killer" or "cleaner" apps**, and don't keep force-stopping apps. They start again by themselves, and
  starting up uses more battery than leaving them alone. Android already rests apps you aren't using.

## 6. Charging

- **Top up whenever there's power**: a power bank, the car (engine running, in the open air), a solar panel, a
  power station.
- A phone charges faster with the **screen off**, and faster still in aeroplane mode.
- **Small solar panels** work best in direct sun, facing it. Through a window they give much less. Charge a power bank
  from the panel during the day, then the phone from the power bank.
- Keep the phone **warm but not hot**: an inside pocket. Cold batteries run down faster (they recover when warm), and a
  hot phone charges slowly.

## Rough numbers

- A typical phone battery holds about 15 to 20 watt-hours. A 10,000 mAh power bank recharges most phones about one and a half times (some energy is lost on the way).
- Sharing ReadyNet from a phone's own hotspot used about **10% battery an hour** with five people joined, in our tests.
  Stop sharing when nobody needs it, and share from a power bank if you can.
- With aeroplane mode on, the screen off and nothing running, many phones lose only a few percent overnight.
